Don’t wait for the link to fail.

FlexiPath keeps a drone’s command-and-control link alive as it flies between Wi-Fi, 5G and LEO satellite. It sees the current path degrading, wakes and checks the next one, protects critical traffic through the handover, and switches before the old link breaks, without resetting a session.

The problem

Today’s devices change networks only after they lose one.

A drone crossing a city, a 5G corridor and a rural zone changes networks several times per flight. A standard operating system stays on the current link until it is gone, then detects the loss, scans for another network, gets a new address and rebuilds every connection.

For a drone, that silence on the C2 link is not an inconvenience. A drone that loses its C2 link falls back to a fail-safe: here, telemetry silent for more than 300 ms makes it stop and hover until the link returns, and the mission waits.

How it works

One decision loop, running ten times a second.

Every 100 ms FlexiPath measures the active path and asks one question: is it about to fail? If it is, the loop prepares the next path, protects what matters for a moment, and moves.

  1. 01
    DETECT

    Measure the active path 10 times a second. Spare modems sleep and report signal only.

    10 Hz · standby 1.2 W
  2. 02
    PREDICT

    A 3-second signal trend projects when the path will reach its failure level.

    8 s look-ahead
  3. 03
    PREPARE

    Wake the best spare modem and validate it with heartbeats before trusting it.

    warm probe 1.8 W
  4. 04
    REPLICATE

    Send critical traffic on both paths for a moment. The first copy to arrive wins.

    400 ms burst · 3.4 W
  5. 05
    SWITCH

    Make the prepared path primary. The old radio goes back to sleep.

    no gap
  6. 06
    CONTINUE

    Sessions ride a tunnel to a fixed anchor relay, so no connection is reset.

    one address

Our solutions

Seven ideas that make the handover invisible.

FlexiPath adapts what already exists, multipath anchoring from MPTCP and 3GPP ATSSS and packet duplication, and adds prediction, a coverage map of the flight plan, sleeping standby radios and protection that lasts only as long as the risk.

Predict the failure

The signal’s trend projects when the path will fail, so FlexiPath acts 1.3–4.6 s before the failure. The legacy stack acts after it.

MEASURED · SIM

Spare radios sleep until needed

Standby modems stay in low-power DRX and wake only when a degradation is predicted. Mean radio power stays at 1.25–1.32 W, against 2.65–3.39 W for always-on duplication.

MEASURED · SIM

Protect only what matters

The C2 link is copied onto both paths only during a 400 ms burst around the handover. Voice joins above its 2 % loss budget. Camera and survey uploads are never duplicated.

MODEL PARAMETER

One address for the whole flight

Every flow rides a UDP tunnel to a static anchor relay. Ground services see one IP across Wi-Fi, 5G and satellite, through carrier NAT: 0 remote-visible address changes.

MEASURED · SIM

No waiting for order

Each critical packet carries a sequence number. The receiver keeps the first copy and drops the late one, so C2 and voice never wait in line: 0 ms head-of-line delay in every run.

MEASURED · SIM

Every decision explained

Each switch comes with its reason: the values FlexiPath measured and the thresholds they crossed, shown live beside the rule the legacy stack follows.

Know the route

A drone flies a planned route, so FlexiPath also reads a coverage map of it, built from earlier survey flights. Where Wi-Fi and 5G die at the same place (an urban canyon, a ridge), no trend can warn in time and the usual backup dies too. FlexiPath sees the dead zone 8 s ahead, wakes the satellite, and is on it before the shadow. If the map is out of date, it pays for a needless move, and a new shadow is handled by fast failover.

Against reactive multipath

FlexiPath loses 25–40 % fewer critical packets, raises voice quality (MOS) by 0.07–0.10 and acts 1.3–4.6 s before the failure instead of 0.3–0.5 s after, for about 0.1 W more radio power.

Against always duplicate

Permanent duplication delivers the most critical packets, 99–100 %, but draws 2.1–2.6× FlexiPath’s radio power and copies 49–100 % of protected bytes, against FlexiPath’s ≈ 1 %. When Wi-Fi and 5G die together (scenario G), its two copies die together too: it kept C2 on 8 of 20 flights, FlexiPath on 20 of 20 (piloted-flight hover rule, below).

The hover rule decides who keeps C2

A drone hovers when its C2 link stays silent too long. Our default, 300 ms, suits a piloted drone: 3GPP TS 22.125 expects a stick command every 40 ms. Under it, FlexiPath is the only stack that keeps C2 on every flight of every scenario. For waypoint flight the standard allows 1 s; then all three multipath stacks keep C2, and FlexiPath’s edge is its shorter gaps, its delivery and its power.

How we know which part does the work

Ablation: one part off at a time

FlexiPath flies again with one part switched off, on the same random streams. Without trend prediction it loses 10–30 more critical packets per flight (A–E) and acts early on 13–40 % of failures instead of 62–100 %. Without the map, the 0.20 s gap comes back in G. Without the 400 ms burst, nothing measurable changes (within ±0.1 pp) and it saves 0.03 W: in this model the gain comes from prediction and preparation.

Checked once on unseen seeds

Every scenario was re-run on a held-out block of seeds never used while building or tuning FlexiPath. The conclusions hold: 0.6–1.1 points more critical packets than reactive multipath (95 % intervals exclude zero), and FlexiPath again kept C2 on every flight.

How good the prediction is

Recall, the failures acted on before they happened: 100 % in A–C, E–G, and 62 % in D, where one outage per flight is abrupt. Precision, the early moves a real failure followed: 97–100 %, falling to 70 % with an out-of-date map (H).

Checked against real-world measurements

PUBLISHED SOURCES · SEE README

The model’s numbers were compared with published measurements: Linux defaults, measured Wi-Fi, 4G and 5G handovers, an LTE modem’s wake-up time and power, and Starlink’s latency and power. One was optimistic: a sleeping LTE modem took 260 ms to wake, not 100 ms. Re-run with 260 ms, FlexiPath’s lead over reactive multipath grows wherever it predicts the failure, but an outage nobody predicts then exceeds the 300 ms piloted budget. Time on the satellite also costs more in reality than in the model: a Starlink Mini draws 20–40 W.

QoS / QoE guidelines

Nine rules for connectivity that never makes users wait.

Each one is backed by the same paired simulation runs, scenarios A–H.

  1. 01

    Anchor the session, not the interface

    A path change should never reset a connection.

  2. 02

    Act on the trend, not on the failure

    Forecast the failure and start moving before it happens.

  3. 03

    Prepare the next path just in time

    Let spare radios sleep; wake and validate one only when needed.

  4. 04

    Duplicate only what needs it

    Protection per traffic class, and only around the handover.

  5. 05

    Deliver the first copy

    Never make real-time traffic wait to be put in order.

  6. 06

    Detect faster than the app can tolerate

    For outages no trend predicts, detection plus wake-up must fit the deadline.

  7. 07

    Hold still unless moving clearly pays

    Hysteresis and dwell times stop ping-pong handovers.

  8. 08

    Measure what users feel, on matched pairs

    Judge experience and cost under identical conditions, not throughput alone.

  9. 09

    Use what you know about the route

    Map known dead zones and prepare before them; keep the map current, because a stale one costs.

From simulation to the field

The real-life plan.

The prototype proves the idea on matched simulated runs. The next steps take the same controller onto real radios, then into the air.

  1. Done

    Simulation prototype

    Deterministic network simulation, 3D digital twin, comparison with two existing designs and three ablations over 160 paired runs, checked on held-out seeds, and QoS/QoE guidelines.

  2. Next

    Hardware in the loop

    Run the same controller on a companion computer with Wi-Fi, 5G and satellite modems, replay recorded signal traces, and measure real handover gaps and radio power.

  3. Proposed

    Field trials

    Instrumented flights on a test corridor: FlexiPath against the stock stack on matched routes, measuring C2 continuity, delivered packets and energy.

  4. Proposed

    Deployment

    A network layer designed to sit under the operator’s C2 application, with the anchor relay at the ground station or the network edge.

What this prototype is, and isn’t

  • A simulation: the radio, satellite and power models are stylised, and nothing here is a field measurement.
  • Packets are still lost: about 1–2 % of critical packets in the runs.
  • An outage no trend can predict still costs a detection gap: 0.16 s in scenario D.
  • FlexiPath uses about 8 % more radio power than a single-radio stack.
  • Drone altitude is visual only; the radio model is evaluated at ground level.
  • The coverage map knows only persistent geography; an out-of-date map costs a needless move (scenario H).
  • In this model the 400 ms duplication burst adds no measurable reliability; prediction and preparation do.
